Survivor data first

Your clients' safety is part of your security model.

Most IT setups assume the worst thing that can happen is a breach. For you, a single exposed address or a forwarded email can put someone in danger. We design for that.

  • Locations stay private

    We keep shelter addresses out of email signatures, shared calendars and device location settings. We also check what photos, Wi-Fi names and online listings give away, and help you get them taken down.

  • Survivor data stays yours

    We don't open client files or mailboxes. Our everyday accounts can't reach them, we use separate admin accounts only when a task needs one, and admin activity is logged in your tenant where you can see it. If a fix ever brings us near client data, we ask first. Your organization owns its data, and we put that in writing.

  • Least access, every login protected

    Advocates see the records their role needs and nothing more. Every account gets MFA, admins included, and conditional access wherever your licenses include it. Nobody shares a login.

  • Support without client details

    A support request never needs a client's name or address. We train staff to describe the problem, not the person. If client information lands in a support email by mistake, we delete it and tell you.

  • Built around your rules

    VAWA and FVPSA limit how survivor information can be shared, and federal law bars victim service providers from entering identifying client data into HMIS, so you use a comparable database instead. We set up your tools to fit those rules and keep our own access as narrow as the work allows.

  • Staff safety counts too

    When an advocate is being harassed or targeted online, we help lock down their accounts and devices. Then we walk them through what was exposed and what to change.

During the assessment we check each of these against your setup and show you what we found. Questions before then? Email us.

How we work

The first ninety days, written down before we start.

It starts with a free assessment. We visit, look around, and give you a written summary whether or not you hire us. If you do, this is the plan.

  1. Days 1–30

    Listen and stabilize

    • Walk every site and meet the staff
    • Turn on MFA for every account, admins first
    • Document what exists, from routers to licenses
    • Take over admin access properly, with nothing left in a former vendor's name
    • Fix anything urgent we find along the way

  2. Days 31–60

    Secure the basics

    • Conditional access and sign-in alerts
    • Backups verified with a real test restore
    • Device encryption and remote wipe
    • Email protection and sender authentication
    • Clean up accounts for people who have left

  3. Days 61–90

    Plan the next year

    • A written roadmap in plain English
    • Costs laid out so they fit your budget and grant cycles
    • Your first quarterly review with leadership

Before day one

Switching from your current provider.

We do the asking. You send one email to your current provider authorizing the handover, and we collect the rest:

  • Microsoft 365 global admin and the transfer of your licenses
  • Your domain registration and DNS
  • Firewall, router and Wi-Fi logins
  • Backup accounts and any documentation they hold

We plan the switch around the notice period in your current contract and work alongside your current provider until every login is in your organization's name. Phones and the crisis line move last, and we test them before anything is switched off.

Before you choose

Questions worth asking any IT partner.

When a staff member needs help, who answers?
One of the two of us. At onboarding your staff get our direct contact details, and we learn who they are and how their office runs.
Will the same person handle it next time?
It'll be one of the same two people every time, and we both know your setup, so nobody has to explain their office again.
How fast do you respond?
Our goals: when something urgent comes in during support hours, one of us is on it within 30 minutes. Everything else gets a reply the same business day. The monthly report shows how we did against both.
Can you explain last month's work without jargon?
That's what the monthly report is for. If a line in it needs a glossary, we rewrite the line.
Is managed IT new for you?
The division is new. The work isn't: we've been setting up, running and maintaining networks, Microsoft 365 and security for a long time. Offering it as a managed service means we take on a small number of clients and both of us stay closely involved. We built it around confidential sites from the start, and in the assessment we'll show you the exact settings we'd use for addresses, Wi-Fi names, devices and calendars. Every login and document sits in your tenant, in your name, from the first month.
What happens to our data if we part ways?
It stays yours. We hand back admin access, passwords and documentation cleanly, and we'll help whoever comes next get started.

Ask anyone you're considering the same questions, us included.

FAQ

Common questions.

What does the free assessment involve?

We start with a call, then visit your main office and any others where it makes sense. We look at your network, Microsoft 365 setup, devices, backups and security settings, and we talk with whoever handles IT today. You get a written summary of what we found and what we'd fix first. It's yours to keep whether or not you hire us.

How is pricing set?

Nonprofit pricing, flat monthly, quoted after the assessment. The number depends on how many people and sites you have, not on how many times you contact us. The quote lists the security and backup tools we use and whether each one is included in the monthly price or billed at cost, so it can go into a grant budget line by line. It also says what would cost extra, like new hardware or a large project.

What are your hours, and what happens after hours?

Regular support runs on weekday business hours, with the exact hours set in your agreement. Outside them we keep an urgent route for a short list of emergencies: phones or the crisis line down, an account that looks compromised, a lost device with client data on it, or signs of ransomware. Those reach both of us at any hour, and the callback goal goes in writing before you sign. Password resets and printer trouble wait until morning. We won't promise a round-the-clock helpdesk that two people can't honestly staff.

Do you only work in Minnesota?

We're based in Sherburne County, northwest of the Twin Cities, and most day-to-day support happens remotely. When something can't be fixed from here, one of us comes to the office. During the assessment we agree how quickly we can reach each of your locations and put it in writing.

Can you work alongside our current provider during a handover?

Yes. We work in parallel for an agreed period, collect admin access and documentation properly, and take over once nothing is left loose. Switching from your current provider covers what we collect.

Do you help with Microsoft nonprofit licensing?

Yes. Microsoft ended its free Business Premium and Office 365 E1 grants for nonprofits, starting at each organization's first renewal after July 1, 2025. Business Basic is still free for up to 300 users, and the paid plans carry nonprofit discounts. Some protections on this page, like conditional access and remote wipe, need Business Premium or an add-on, and we'll show you the cost before recommending it. We check what you have, what you pay for and what your staff actually use, then recommend the smallest setup that covers your security needs.

What happens if one of you is out sick?

The other one covers. We both work in your environment, keep shared documentation of your setup, and hold the access needed to step in. If one of us will be away for a planned stretch, you'll hear about it ahead of time.
